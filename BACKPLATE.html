2Zone Notes

Top — AC Entry & PSU

The PSU is pushed into the enclosure's top-right corner, long-axis horizontal, top and right sides against the enclosure walls (vents stay clear). It's larger than the room below it, so it rides on standoffs and overhangs the backplate's top-right edge — the standoffs are also sized to clear the hardware that fixes the backplate to the enclosure. Its terminal end faces left/inward so the Romex and the DC output runs land toward the plate, not against a wall.
  • Direct Romex (NM-B). The enclosure is recessed in the wall, so building AC is run to it as NM-B cable. It enters a top-wall knockout through an NM cable clamp and lands on the PSU's L / N / ⏚ terminals (the hot via an inline fuse holder first). No IEC inlet, no cord-and-plug. See "AC Entry & In-Wall Rough-In" in Build Tips.

Distribution — POWER & SIGNAL Blocks

Two OONO 2×12 distribution blocks, 70 × 48 mm each. The POWER block sits just below the PSU's DC terminals — A rail = +24V, B rail = Power GND — so the fat PSU V+/V− wires drop straight down. The SIGNAL block is rotated 90° into the narrow strip left of the PSU (hinge side, where the panel umbilical lands) — A rail = switch commons, B rail = device GND. One jumper bridges Power GND → Signal GND: the star ground, the only tie between the two ground domains, which keeps PWM-switching noise off the ADC reads. The buck converter shares the distribution band, right of the POWER block.

MOSFET Row — 3 Boards

Three 55 × 55 mm 4-channel boards in a row across the lower backplate, on M3 standoffs. Orient each so the control edge (PWM-in + VIN + GND) faces up toward the ESP32 and POWER block, and the output edge faces down — strip-cable runs loop down and around the inside wall up to the top-wall exit. VIN/GND tap to the POWER block; the 10 PWM inputs come from the ESP32.

ESP32 — Below the PSU, Hinge Side

The 38-pin ESP32 + screw-terminal adapter mounts directly below the PSU on the left/hinge side. It's sandwiched between the PSU and the MOSFET row, so USB-C points left toward the open hinge side — the only short edge with clearance (up and down are blocked within ~5–8 mm). Its screw-terminal rows then run along the top and bottom edges: the bottom row faces the MOSFETs, so the 10 PWM wires drop straight down with no bend. The panel umbilical arrives from the left and fans to the two rows.
  • All 9 panel switches/button wire directly to ESP32 GPIOs — no I/O expander. See CLAUDE.md for the pin map.
  • The 4-wire I²C bus (SDA / SCL / 3V3 / GND) leaves the ESP32 and crosses the door hinge to the two displays — CCT and lumens.
  • Switch commons, pot lows, ESP32 GND and the display GND return all land on the SIGNAL block beside it (~13 landings across its two rails) rather than chaining onto one strip.

4Cable Entries (Enclosure Walls, Not Backplate)

The hinged door is on the left. The Concord panels are mounted to the door, so panel cables cross the hinge as a flexible umbilical — no external cable gland needed for them.

Top Wall — Right Side: AC Entry

Direct Romex (NM-B). A 14/2 NM-B cable from the branch circuit enters a knockout through an NM cable clamp/connector — the clamp is the strain relief, purpose-made for flat NM cable (not a gland, not a bare hole). Inside, the hot passes through an inline fuse holder before the PSU's L terminal.
Position over the PSU's terminal end so the AC run is short. A ½″ NM clamp wants a ~⅞″ knockout — step bit. See "AC Entry & In-Wall Rough-In" in Build Tips.

Top Wall — Left Side: Strip Cable Bundle

1× large strain relief / multi-cable bushing. Five 18/3 cables exit together, bundled with split-loom or spiral wrap, then run up the wall through a single channel/conduit to the joists.
For a ~5-cable bundle of 18/3 (~6–8 mm OD each), look at: Heyco SR-7P-4 multi-port strain relief, a 1″ NPT cord grip with multi-cable insert, or a single large rubber grommet (1″ – 1.25″ ID) plus an internal cable clamp for strain relief.

Left Wall — Door Hinge Crossover

Panel cables (3 cables, or one bundled umbilical) cross from the door panels to the backplate via a flexible service loop near the hinge.
No gland required — keep the cable bundle inside the enclosure. Anchor on both the door side and the backplate side; leave enough slack so the door swings fully open without tension. See zone note below for routing detail.

Right & Bottom Walls — Unused

No penetrations. Bottom kept clear so the box can rest on this side during assembly & wiring.
Reserves space for future expansion if you ever need an external sensor input, remote enable, or aux output.

7Notes & Build Tips

Wire Color Convention

AC: black (Line), white (Neutral), green (Ground) — US/NEC standard.
DC power: red (+24V), black (0V/GND).
Signal: any high-contrast color. Yellow and blue are easy on the eyes for switch wires and pot wipers.

Fuse Sizing

LRS-450-24 draws ~4 A peak at 120 V AC (450 W ÷ 0.9 efficiency ÷ 120 V). A 5 A slow-blow 5×20 mm fuse in the panel-mount inline holder — ahead of the PSU's L terminal — covers normal operation and tolerates PSU inrush at startup. Don't use fast-blow; it'll nuisance-trip on power-up.

AC Entry & In-Wall Rough-In

The enclosure is recessed in the wall and fed by direct Romex (NM-B) — a permanent hardwired connection.
  • Keep the box accessible. A PSU and splices live in here — don't drywall over it. Trim the wall opening so the door sits flush and swings fully open.
  • Heat. Real load is well under the PSU's 450 W (LED draw ~150–300 W) and the box is oversized, so a closed in-wall box is fine — just don't pack insulation against it. Add vent holes top + bottom into the stud bay if it ever runs warm.
  • Romex routing. Run the NM-B continuously from its source to the box, in through a top-wall knockout on an NM cable clamp (that clamp is the strain relief). Staple within ~12″ of the box and every ~4½ ft; protect it at framing pass-throughs with setback or a nail plate.
  • Inline fuse + ground. The hot passes through the 5 A inline fuse holder before the PSU's L terminal. The NM-B ground lands on the chassis ground bond and pigtails to the PSU's ⏚.
  • The 18/3 strip runs. Five 18/3 cables exit the top of the box and run up the wall cavity to the ceiling joists. Keep them off the NM-B run (separate holes, a little spacing where parallel), support every ~3–4 ft, and label each (Strip 1–5) before fishing. Staple to the framing or pull through the FMC in the shopping list — your call.

Chassis Ground Bond

The AC green/ground wire must bond to the enclosure metal. Don't rely on the NM cable clamp's locknut alone — that's for mechanical fit, not a guaranteed electrical bond.
  • Use an M6 star washer (external tooth) — the teeth bite through any factory paint into bare metal. A spring/split washer alone won't do this reliably.
  • Wire termination: strip ~¼″ of the green wire, crimp a pre-insulated M6 ring terminal onto it using regular pliers (squeeze the metal barrel flat onto the wire strands). Stack: enclosure → star washer → ring terminal → screw. Tighten firmly.
  • Optional belt+suspenders: add an M6 spring washer above the ring terminal for vibration retention. Not needed for a static enclosure but cheap insurance.

Verify Concord Button LED Before Wiring

The panel label reads "5–12V DC LED LIGHT." A bare LED can't accept that voltage range, so the button almost certainly has internal current limiting. But verify before powering it from the 5V driver-module output:
  • Multimeter check: set to diode-test mode, measure across the LED terminals both ways. If you see ~2V drop one direction and OL the other, plus a few kΩ resistance on the resistance setting — it has internal limiting. Drive it directly from the LED driver module's OUT+ / OUT−.
  • If it measures like a bare LED (~0.6V diode drop, very low resistance) — you'd need to add a ~470 Ω resistor in series with the LED. Easiest no-solder approach: use two heat-shrink butt-splice connectors with the resistor leads in between, inline on the wire run.
  • Either way, the driver module wiring (VCC to 5V, GND to Power GND, SIG to GPIO 15) stays the same.

Door Hinge Cable Management

Panels + display are on the door; their wiring crosses the hinge as a single flexible umbilical.
  • Bundle the 3 panel cables + 4-wire display I²C run into one flexible umbilical with spiral wrap or fabric sleeving — easier on the hinge than 4 loose cables.
  • Service loop: route from each panel + display toward the hinge, then a loose arc across to the backplate. With the door open ~110°, there should be zero tension. With the door closed, the slack tucks behind the door frame.
  • Anchor on both sides: cable clamp / adhesive mount on the door's interior near the hinge, and another on the backplate near where the cables reach the ESP32. Anchors absorb the flex stress so wire-to-terminal joints aren't getting tugged on every door swing.
  • Optional but recommended: terminate panel pigtails with a small in-line connector (JST-XH or Dupont) near the hinge crossing. That way the door can be removed for service without unsoldering anything.

I²C Bus Wiring (two displays)

Both 4-digit displays sit on one I²C bus. A 4-wire run (SDA / SCL / 3V3 / GND) leaves the ESP32, crosses the door hinge to display #1 (CCT), then a short 4-wire jumper daisy-chains to display #2 (lumens).
  • Pins: SDA = GPIO 21, SCL = GPIO 22. Two displays cost no extra GPIOs — I²C is a bus.
  • Addresses: CCT display = 0x70 (default, jumpers open). Lumens display = 0x71 — bridge its A0 address jumper. Label both before mounting.
  • Pull-ups: Each Adafruit backpack has on-board ~10 kΩ I²C pull-ups; with two backpacks they sit in parallel (~5 kΩ effective) — still fine for this short 100 kHz bus. Do not add external pull-ups.
  • Voltage: 3.3 V. No level shifter needed (the backpacks also accept 5 V via VCC).
  • Wire colors (convention): SDA = yellow, SCL = blue, 3V3 = red, GND = black. Use these consistently across the umbilical so the bus is visually traceable.
  • Bus length: Backplate-to-displays run is <2 ft total — well within I²C's practical limit at 100 kHz. If you ever see comms errors, drop to 50 kHz in firmware before adding hardware.

Displays: Mounting & Bezels

Both displays live on the door alongside the Concord panels — part of the panel array, not the backplate. Mount them as a matched pair, a small instrument cluster.
  • Window cutouts: Each 0.56″ Adafruit backpack reads cleanly behind a rectangular window ~30 mm wide × 14 mm tall. Cut two — one per display — and deburr the edges.
  • The labels: A permanent label beside each window — "K" for the CCT display, "lm" for the lumens display. Options: (a) vinyl cutter / Cricut + matte black vinyl (cheapest, removable); (b) laser-engraved aluminum bezel ring (most premium); (c) 3D-printed bezel with raised text; (d) screen-printed if you're sending the panel out anyway. Authentic Apollo-era panels labeled their numeric windows externally — a lit-up unit label would not read as more correct.
  • Brightness: The HT16K33 has 16 levels (0–15). Default is max — usually too bright for a dim room. display.setBrightness(3) reads as "instrument lit," not "screen on." Match both displays.
  • Mounting: Either through-panel via M2.5 standoffs (cleanest, requires precise cutout alignment) or foam-tape behind a bezel ring (forgiving, works regardless of cutout precision). Foam tape is fine — the displays weigh almost nothing.
  • Off behavior: When master_on is false, blank both displays via display.clear(); display.writeDisplay();. The dark windows double as a power-status indicator.

Computing the Readouts

Both numbers are computed, not measured (see CLAUDE.md for the canonical formulas).

CCT — the warm/cool mix one fully-enabled bay would emit:

warm_eff = warm_bias  * warm_enable
cool_eff = cool_bias  * cool_enable
total    = warm_eff + cool_eff
if total == 0:
    cct_display.blank()
else:
    cct = 3200 + (cool_eff / total) * (6500 - 3200)
    cct_display.print(round(cct))

Lumens — the whole-system total across all 10 PWM channels:

LUMENS_FULL_PER_CHANNEL = 2310      # 15.4 m installed × 750 lm/m per color ÷ 5 strips
total_lm = sum( (duty / duty_max) * LUMENS_FULL_PER_CHANNEL
                for each of the 10 PWM channels )
if (not master_on) or total_lm == 0:
    lm_display.blank()
elif total_lm < 10000:
    lm_display.print(total_lm)          # 1..9999, no decimal point
else:
    lm_display.print(total_lm / 10000)  # 23,100 -> "2.310", decimal point lit (system max)
Constants 3200 / 6500 K (the FilmGrade Hybrid strip's tungsten + daylight endpoints per datasheet PN 3002.HY) and LUMENS_FULL_PER_CHANNEL are derived nominals — tune against a meter once the strips are installed. The CCT readout reflects one fully-enabled bay (bay-by-bay differences don't change it); the lumens readout is the actual summed output of all five bays.

Door Clearance Check

The Concord switches/pots/buttons project ~1–1.5″ behind the panels into the enclosure interior when the door is closed. With 6″ enclosure depth minus 1.2″ PSU minus ~1.5″ door projection, you've got ~3.3″ of clear airspace in the middle — plenty for wire routing. Just keep tall components (ESP32 + adapter on standoffs ≈ 2″) off the direct path behind the panels; the layout above puts the ESP32 well below the panel area.

Future-Proof Capacity

  • MOSFET channels: MOS-3 has 2 spare channels — enough to drive a 6th LED strip or a separate accent channel.
  • Distribution strips: 12-pos blocks give ~2 spares each for +24V and Power GND.
  • ESP32 GPIOs: All 24 usable GPIOs are allocated on the 38-pin board. To add another sensor or output later, hang it on the existing I²C bus (the two displays sit there today; plenty of bus capacity left). An MCP23017 expander or any I²C peripheral would slot in cleanly without re-wiring anything.

Upgrade Path: Bus Bars

If you later want the boutique-aerospace look inside the enclosure, swap the +24V and Power GND euro strips for marine-style tinned copper bus bars (Blue Sea 2300-series or similar). Same role, fancier hardware. Requires adding a ring-terminal crimper to your kit.
